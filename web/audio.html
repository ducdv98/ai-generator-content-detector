How the marks work

File labels. A WAV or MP3 holds the sound plus blocks of notes: ID3 tags, XMP metadata, C2PA Content Credentials. Any service that decodes the sound and writes a new file leaves them behind.

Sound watermark. The key assigns +1 or −1 to each of 550 narrow pitch bands. A filter makes each band very slightly louder or quieter to match, which in time is a pattern of faint echoes just after every sound. The mark is the music itself, re-coloured, so it is loud only where the music is loud enough to hide it. The writer measures the clip first and only moves bands the music does not already have on the right side.

Reading it back. The detector compares each band with its neighbours, averages over time, and correlates with the key. The music's own fine structure changes from note to note and averages away; the pattern does not. Because the pattern is the same at every instant, trimming, cutting and tempo changes do not matter. Anything that moves pitch slides the sound off the pattern.

What vendors deploy (not runnable here). Meta's open AudioSeal and Google's SynthID for audio train an encoder network to write the mark and a detector network to find it, shaped by the sound moment to moment. More detail and sources: docs/audio-watermarking.md.
